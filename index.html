What we believe

Same chemistry. Same tunnel. Same POS. Very different results.

Operators across this industry buy from similar chemistry programs, similar equipment makers, similar technology categories, and increasingly similar business models. The performance gap shows up in the daily operating friction around all of it.

How work actually flows. How decisions get made and who makes them. How well the technology fits the workflow it's supposed to serve. Which behaviors get reinforced, where the handoffs between teams lose something, and how consistently the whole system holds across dozens or hundreds of locations.

The friction is often familiar. The harder part is getting underneath it.

Most enterprise teams can name several things that aren't working. The work is understanding why they persist, where the leverage sits, and what change is practical enough to actually hold.

The IgniteDrive Loop

Test the idea before asking the organization to scale it.

FIG. 02 The IgniteDrive Loop iterative,
not linear
01 Observe
02 Frame
03 Design
04 Pilot
05 Learn & scale

The dashed path is real: what a pilot teaches often sends the work back to Frame or Design before anything scales.

01

Observe

Get close to the work. Understand how the system operates today and hear from the people living inside it.

02

Frame

Get underneath the friction and develop a clear hypothesis about the problem and where leverage may exist.

03

Design

Create a practical intervention — technology, workflow, behavior system, operating model, or some combination.

04

Pilot

Test the idea with a deliberately chosen set of locations, before the broader organization is asked to make a larger bet.

05

Learn & scale

Measure what changed, sharpen the approach, and decide honestly whether it deserves a broader rollout.

Not every engagement runs all five stages. It's how we prefer to work when the problem and the estate allow for it — enterprise problems don't always require enterprise-wide bets.

What the Loop is looking for

Find where the leverage is.

That's the whole objective of the Loop. Not every point of friction matters equally: some is noise, and some compounds. A workaround repeated across a hundred locations isn't small anymore, and small execution gaps become permanent operating cost.

So Observe and Frame exist to find the places where technology, behavior, workflow, or operating systems create meaningful drag — and Design and Pilot exist to prove a practical change can move it.

The cost of doing nothing is rarely zero. It's just harder to see on a P&L.

Some problems stay stuck for a reason.

Enterprise operators rarely lack capable people or good ideas. The hardest problems persist because they cross functions, carry legacy complexity, compete with daily operating priorities, or need focused experimentation the normal cadence can't absorb.

We bring dedicated focus, field research, outside pattern recognition, and a test-and-learn process to move one of them forward.

Outside perspective

Your operation, seen in industry context.

Your team has depth — nobody understands your operation, your history, or your constraints better. What no internal team can have is breadth, because no operator gets to sit inside anyone else's organization.

That's the part we add: a vantage point built from years inside enterprise car washing — operator relationships, product work, field research, and the questions leaders keep bringing us. Which approaches are being tested elsewhere, which technology bets are being made, what has quietly failed, and whether your problem is common or genuinely yours.

Patterns travel. Confidential information doesn't.

Questions we can help answer
Is this problem common in the industry, or specific to us? How are other sophisticated operators structuring this function? What's being tried with technology right now — and what hasn't worked? What do better-performing operators seem to do differently here? Where has this approach failed elsewhere, and why?

Breadth across the industry, depth protected inside it: we carry patterns between operators, never confidential information. Every engagement makes the next one smarter — and no engagement exposes the last one.
