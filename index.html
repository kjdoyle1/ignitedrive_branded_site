The technology decision problem

The bigger the operation, the more expensive the wrong technology decision becomes.

Scale compounds all of it: software cost, integration complexity, migration risk, and the operational consequences of getting it wrong. A workaround at three sites is an annoyance; at a hundred it’s a line item.

The calls also get less reversible — migrate or stay, buy or build, consolidate or integrate — and they’re usually made from vendor material rather than from the operation the technology has to live inside.

Technology works inside an operating system — people, workflow, management, capacity.

Which is why the best software on paper can still be the wrong answer. Buy, build, integrate, replace, pilot — or leave it alone — only becomes a real choice once you understand the workflows, the people, and the workarounds already there.

Where conversations usually start

Bring us a technology problem.

Most of these start as a sentence someone says out loud in a leadership meeting.

“We’re thinking about changing POS.”

Requirements from the workflow up, an honest comparison, and what migration will really take.

“Our tech stack has become too expensive.”

What you own, what overlaps, what nobody uses, and which gaps are worth paying to close.

“We don’t trust what we’re hearing in demos.”

Diligence on what vendors don’t lead with: implementation, support, data, and your edge cases.

“We’re growing fast and our systems may not scale.”

What breaks between 20 and 100 locations, and what the stack has to look like before you get there.

“We want to use AI but don’t know where it creates leverage.”

One use case, one bounded environment, one honest read on whether it produced value.

“We think we need a technology leader — but what should that role own?”

What the work really is, what should be internal, and what shouldn’t be hired for at all.
